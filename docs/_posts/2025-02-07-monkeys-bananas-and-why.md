<p>Once in a while, it happens when I come across a situation where people do things without truly understanding why they do what they do. To lighten the mood after a series of 'Spanish inquisition' why questions, I like to tell this anecdote.</p>

<figure class="wp-block-image aligncenter size-full is-resized"><img src="/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/DALL·E-2025-02-07-08.31.06-A-colorful-cartoon-illustration-of-a-monkey-at-the-top-of-a-ladder-inside-a-cage-being-sprayed-with-cold-water-from-above.-The-monkey-looks-surprised.webp" alt="" class="wp-image-119" style="width:382px;height:auto"/></figure>

<!--more-->

<p>5 monkeys are placed in a cage with a ladder, with a tempting bunch of bananas at its top.</p>

<p>Every time a monkey attempts to climb the ladder, the experimenter sprays the entire cage with icy water.</p>

<p>To avoid the cold shock, the monkeys quickly learn to pull down any monkey that dares to climb.</p>

<p>Then, one by one, each original monkey is replaced with a newcomer.</p>

<p>The new monkey, unaware of the rule and the icy water, instinctively tries to climb for the bananas.</p>

<p>After suffering a beating from his peers, the new monkey learns not to climb—even though he’s never been sprayed.</p>

<p>Gradually, all the original monkeys are replaced, yet the behavior remains unchanged.</p>

<p></p>

<p>This story mirrors how organizational cultures can operate:</p>

<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Practices and policies continue simply because “that’s how it’s always been done.”</li>

<li>People follow outdated rules without questioning their relevance or purpose.</li>
</ul>
